Overview

My first released Unity game

Light in the Dark began as an early attempt to build a complete game rather than an isolated mechanic. The player survives an escalating horde by moving, aiming, shooting, managing health, and selecting upgrades.

The project required the core loop, enemy variety, progression, interface, art, menus, and release process to work together. It became the foundation for many of the systems-oriented decisions I make in larger projects today.

Design challenge

Creating variety from a simple foundation

The primary mechanic is intentionally straightforward: move, aim, and fire while surviving a growing horde. The challenge was making that loop remain interesting as pressure increased.

I used enemy archetypes, wave pacing, and upgrades to change how the player evaluates space and danger. The project taught me that readable differences in behavior are often more useful than simply adding more objects or statistics.

Core gameplay loop

  1. 1Enter a wave
  2. 2Read enemy behaviors
  3. 3Survive and improve
  4. 4Face stronger pressure

Takeaways

Lessons carried into later work

Finish the whole loop

Menus, controls, difficulty, feedback, and release preparation matter as much as the central mechanic.

Build readable enemies

Distinct silhouettes and behaviors help players make decisions while the screen becomes busy.

Use progression deliberately

Upgrades are most satisfying when they noticeably change the player’s ability to manage pressure.
